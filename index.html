02 gameprice

Why does the same game cost a different amount depending on where and how you buy it?

I could not answer that without opening six tabs, so I built the thing that answers it in one.

What it does

  • Search a game and see Steam and GOG side by side
  • Flags which store is cheaper and by how much
  • Pulls prices live at the moment you search, not from a stored table
  • Ranks a game's five cheapest Steam regions
  • Always shows Saudi Arabia as a reference row, even when it is not in the top five
  • Browses current Steam discounts and GOG offers, grouped by genre
  • Lists games that are temporarily free to claim
  • Saves favourites, switches currency, switches between dark and light
  • Links straight out to the real store page

03 huí

Huí 回

Experimental. In development.

A Chinese-learning project I keep taking apart and rebuilding. It is also where the shape framing this page came from.

01 the idea

What if a word arrived attached to a place, instead of to a card?

Flashcards taught me to recognise 前面. They did not teach me to notice it. So I started building the version where you have to walk past it.

02 what exists

  1. The exchange

    Someone says something to you and you answer. The reply then tells you why your answer worked. Hanzi, pinyin, English and Arabic sit together, because the second translation is where meaning usually falls out.

    Built · runs in a browser

  2. Sound, and keeping score honestly

    Listening, pronunciation, and a record of every word across four separate abilities — recognise it, hear it, recall it, say it — rather than one number pretending to be mastery.

    Built · 464 automated tests passing

  3. The world

    The same idea moved into Unreal Engine: a walkable residential garden where a word is attached to the place it describes. It packages, it runs, and it holds sixty frames a second at the ninety-ninth percentile. It is also one small place and one short visit.

    Prototype · in development, not released

The three screens below are from the packaged Unreal build, captured at 2560×1440 and not retouched. It is a prototype rather than a product, and the interface still says so out loud.

03 what it taught me

  • Whether a learner can pick a word out of a noisy street depends far more on the listening conditions than on whether they know the word. I only found that because I measured it instead of assuming it.
  • The reference audio is still not good enough, so nothing in the project is allowed to grade a learner's pronunciation yet. Shipping a confident-looking score on top of placeholder audio would have been the easy version.
  • Performance work is mostly disproven guesses. The setting I was most confident about made the frame time worse, three runs in a row; the real cost turned out to be a mis-sized sign sitting on the main sight-line.
  • Three visual directions were built and thrown away before this one. The learning logic underneath survived all three, which is how I found out which part was actually the valuable part.
  • I build this with AI assistance, and the rule I keep is that I only keep the parts I can still check myself — which is why there are tests, captures and measurements here rather than screenshots of a good day.

Two stages run in a browser and a third was prototyped in Unreal Engine. None of it is released, so there is no link to give you yet. It stays on this page because unfinished work is still work, and this is the project teaching me the most.
